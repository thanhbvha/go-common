## Resource Review

- Redis post-drain heap was 2.64-6.70 MB with five lifecycle goroutines. The 1m
  cells peaked at 12.20-12.46 MB heap; no retained backlog owner was present.
- NATS post-drain heap was 2.99-7.77 MB with 8-10 lifecycle goroutines. The
  accepted delayed 1m rerun used resource-only periodic sampling and mandatory
  final exact stats. No blocked KV watcher callback was present.
- Kafka retry cells post-drain heap was 3.42-7.34 MB. Immediate/delayed preload
  cells reached backlog-proportional 180.87-537.20 MB peaks and returned to
  3.42-7.51 MB after drain. Post-drain goroutines were 33-36 expected Kafka
  clients, consumers, delay-tier pollers, and health-check lifecycle routines.
- Allocated broker volume remained finite in every cell and project-scoped
  cleanup removed each named volume.

## Production Caveats

- NATS delayed 1m throughput remains nonlinear and requires topology sampling to
  stay disabled during measurement because exact live-KV enumeration perturbs
  the workload.
- Kafka delayed throughput remains near 205-209 messages/second at both 100k and
  1m, a stable scheduler ceiling that requires production tuning.
- Kafka immediate/delayed preload verification has a large backlog-proportional
  client working set even though retained post-drain heap is small.
- Artifact `broker.log` files produced before this review were captured after
  startup, not at the end of workload execution. Correctness acceptance therefore
  relies on final broker topology state, resource samples, process results, and
  cleanup rather than a claim that historical runtime logs were exhaustively
  reviewed. Both orchestrators now refresh scoped broker logs immediately before
  teardown for future runs.

PowerShell/Windows execution and broader payload/concurrency dimensions are
